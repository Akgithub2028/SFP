\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Continual learning and catastrophic forgetting.}
Catastrophic forgetting---the tendency of neural networks to lose previously learned knowledge when trained on new tasks---has been studied since \citet{mccloskey1989catastrophic} and \citet{french1999catastrophic}.
The continual learning literature organizes mitigations into regularization-based, replay-based, and architecture-based families~\citep{delange2021continual}.
SFP draws from all three: it regularizes activations (not weights), uses a small memory buffer for basis construction, and operates within a fixed architecture.

\paragraph{Regularization methods.}
Elastic Weight Consolidation~\citep[EWC;][]{kirkpatrick2017overcoming} penalizes changes to parameters deemed important via the diagonal Fisher information matrix.
Synaptic Intelligence~\citep{zenke2017continual} computes importance online from the path integral of gradients.
These methods operate in parameter space.
SFP instead operates on a low-rank subspace of the activation space, which is more directly tied to model behavior: two parameter configurations can produce identical activations, but identical activations produce identical outputs.

\paragraph{Knowledge distillation.}
Learning without Forgetting~\citep[LwF;][]{li2017learning} distills the old model's output logits into the new model.
PODNet~\citep{douillard2020podnet} extends distillation to intermediate feature maps, penalizing changes in pooled hidden-state statistics across all dimensions.
SFP is related to PODNet in that both preserve intermediate representations, but SFP is \emph{selective}: it penalizes drift in only the $r$ dimensions that matter most for old-task performance (typically $r = 32$ out of $d = 1536$), leaving the complementary subspace free for new-task learning.

\paragraph{Replay and memory.}
Experience Replay~\citep{chaudhry2019tiny} maintains a small buffer of old-task examples and interleaves them during training.
A-GEM~\citep{chaudhry2018efficient} uses the buffer to project gradients away from directions that increase old-task loss.
SFP uses a memory buffer only for basis construction---extracting PCA directions and computing ablation importance---not for direct training.
The buffer is used once to build the basis, then discarded.

\paragraph{Architectural methods.}
Progressive Neural Networks~\citep{rusu2016progressive} allocate new parameters per task.
Orthogonal LoRA methods~\citep{qin2024exploring} constrain low-rank adapter updates to be orthogonal to the subspace of previous adapters.
SFP shares the geometric intuition---learning should occur in a subspace complementary to important features---but identifies the important subspace empirically via ablation rather than imposing it through adapter orthogonality.

\paragraph{Mechanistic interpretability.}
Recent work on sparse autoencoders~\citep{bricken2023monosemanticity, cunningham2023sparse} and activation patching~\citep{meng2022locating, wang2023interpretability} has shown that individual activation directions can encode interpretable, causally relevant features.
SFP's ablation importance ranking is conceptually related to activation patching: both assess the causal role of activation directions by measuring downstream effects of interventions.

\paragraph{Formal verification in machine learning.}
Formal verification of neural network properties has focused on robustness certification~\citep{katz2017reluplex, wong2018provable}.
To our knowledge, no prior continual learning method has its core mathematical reduction fully machine-checked in a proof assistant.
Our Lean~4 formalization~\citep{mathlib2020} verifies the complete chain from the empirical hypothesis to a forgetting bound, making the logical structure transparent and the assumptions explicit.
