\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented Sparse Feature Preservation (SFP), a method for mitigating catastrophic forgetting in LLMs by penalizing activation drift in a small, empirically identified subspace of important feature dimensions.

\paragraph{Summary.}
At 1.5B parameters, drift in top-$r$ ablation-ranked dimensions predicts forgetting with $R^2 = 0.933$, outperforming random ($0.850$) and bottom-ranked ($0.827$) dimensions.
A causal intervention test confirms that ablation importance identifies causally important features: perturbing top-$r$ dimensions causes $4\times$ more degradation than random dimensions, even at 135M where the correlational test does not hold.
The Lean~4 formalization establishes a clean conditional reduction---assuming H1, bounding $\Lpres \leq \delta$ implies forgetting $\leq C\sqrt{\delta}$---with zero \texttt{sorry} axioms.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Scale.} H1 does not hold at 135M under our protocol. We have not tested at 7B+.

    \item \textbf{LoRA confound.} All experiments use LoRA, which constrains updates to a low-rank subspace. This may artificially reduce the dimensionality of drift. Full fine-tuning experiments are needed.

    \item \textbf{The constant $C$.} The bound $C\sqrt{\delta}$ depends on an empirically estimated $C$. If $C$ is large, the bound is vacuous.

    \item \textbf{Task coverage.} H1 validation uses one task pair (Math$\to$Code). Different pairs may exhibit different forgetting geometries.

    \item \textbf{Single seed.} Results are from a single random seed. Confidence intervals across seeds are not yet available.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Future work.}
(1)~Scaling H1 to 7B+ models, where forgetting is a more pressing practical concern.
(2)~Systematic comparison of the gradient-informed basis (SVD of $\partial \mathcal{L}_{\text{old}} / \partial a$) against PCA.
(3)~Feature interpretability (H3): determining whether top-$r$ preserved features are semantically coherent.
(4)~Multi-task sequences with cumulative preserved subspaces.
(5)~Combining SFP with replay for complementary stability--plasticity benefits.
