\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We evaluate two claims: (1)~H1---that forgetting is predicted by drift in importance-ranked activation dimensions (\cref{sec:h1_validation}), and (2)~that top-ranked dimensions are causally important for old-task performance (\cref{sec:causal}).
We also report a dimensionality sweep (\cref{sec:rsweep}).

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Models.}
We evaluate at two scales: SmolLM2-135M-Instruct (135M parameters, CPU) and Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct (1.5B parameters, A10G GPU).
Both are fine-tuned with LoRA~\citep{hu2022lora} (rank 32, $\alpha = 64$, targets: \texttt{q\_proj}, \texttt{v\_proj}).

\paragraph{Tasks.}
Our primary setting is Math$\to$Code: the model is first evaluated on GSM8K~\citep{cobbe2021training}, then fine-tuned on MBPP~\citep{austin2021program} for 500 steps with checkpoints every 25 steps (20 evaluation points).
Forgetting is measured as the increase in math perplexity after code fine-tuning.

\paragraph{SFP configuration.}
Probe layers at $L/4$, $L/2$, $3L/4$.
PCA with $k = 128$ components, top $r = 32$ dimensions by ablation importance, memory set of 128 examples, seed 42.

\paragraph{Predictors.}
We compare four subspaces as forgetting predictors: \textbf{top-$r$} (ablation-ranked), \textbf{total} (all 128 dims), \textbf{random} (uniformly sampled $r$ dims), and \textbf{bottom-$r$} (least important).
$R^2$ is computed via linear regression of forgetting on projected drift across checkpoints.

\subsection{H1 Validation: Correlational Evidence}
\label{sec:h1_validation}

\paragraph{1.5B results.}
\Cref{tab:h1_results} reports $R^2$ values at the 1.5B scale.
Top-$r$ achieves $R^2 = 0.933$, outperforming total ($0.888$), random ($0.850$), and bottom ($0.827$).
The ranking matches H1's prediction exactly: top $>$ total $>$ random $>$ bottom.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\caption{$R^2$ of linear regression predicting forgetting from activation drift (Qwen2.5-1.5B, Math$\to$Code, 500 steps, 20 checkpoints). H1 predicts: top $>$ total $>$ random $>$ bottom.}
\label{tab:h1_results}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
Predictor & Dims & $R^2$ \\
\midrule
Top-$r$ (ablation) & 32 & \textbf{0.933} \\
Total drift & 128 & 0.888 \\
Random dims & 32 & 0.850 \\
Bottom-$r$ (ablation) & 32 & 0.827 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Against our pre-registered gate: (i)~$R^2(\text{top-}r) = 0.933 > 0.6$~\checkmark; (ii)~$R^2(\text{top-}r) > R^2(\text{total})$~\checkmark; (iii)~$R^2(\text{top-}r) \gg R^2(\text{random})$---the gap is $0.083$, which is in the correct direction but modest~($\times$).
We score this as a \textbf{partial pass} (2/3).

\paragraph{135M results.}
At 135M, H1 does not hold: the ranking is inverted, with bottom-ranked dimensions ($R^2 = 0.994$) outperforming top-ranked ($0.913$).
We do not have a confirmed explanation for this inversion.
Possible contributing factors include the small activation dimension ($d = 576$), minimal PCA ($k = 16$, $r = 8$), and only 5 data points from 40 training steps.
This negative result highlights that H1 is scale-dependent under our protocol.

\paragraph{Baseline math performance.}
The pre-fine-tuning math accuracy for Qwen2.5-1.5B is 44.44\% on GSM8K (9 test questions), with baseline perplexity 0.505.
SmolLM2-135M achieves 0\% accuracy (baseline perplexity 1.177).

\subsection{H1 Validation: Causal Evidence}
\label{sec:causal}

Correlation does not establish causation.
To test whether top-$r$ dimensions are \emph{causally} important for old-task performance, we inject calibrated Gaussian noise into specific activation subspaces at inference time and measure the resulting perplexity increase.

\paragraph{Protocol.}
For each dimension set (top-$r$, random, bottom-$r$), we add noise $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 I_r)$ to the corresponding PCA components at each probe layer during evaluation.
$\sigma$ is calibrated to the mean standard deviation of the projected activations, so perturbation magnitudes are comparable across dimension sets.

\paragraph{Results.}
\Cref{tab:causal} reports results at 135M (CPU smoke test with $r = 2$, $k = 4$):

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\caption{Causal intervention: perplexity increase when injecting noise into different dimension sets (SmolLM2-135M, Math task, $1\sigma$ noise scale). Higher $\Delta$ppl = greater causal importance.}
\label{tab:causal}
\begin{tabular}{lc}
\toprule
Dimension set & $\Delta$ppl \\
\midrule
Top-$r$ (ablation) & $+0.85$ \\
Random & $+0.21$ \\
Bottom-$r$ (ablation) & $+0.02$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Perturbing top-$r$ dimensions causes $4\times$ more degradation than random and $42\times$ more than bottom-ranked dimensions.
This result is notable because it holds at 135M, where the correlational H1 test does not hold.
The discrepancy suggests that ablation importance correctly identifies causally important dimensions even at small scale, but the \emph{correlation} between drift in those dimensions and forgetting is confounded---possibly because drift in all dimensions contributes to forgetting when the model's capacity is limited.

\subsection{Dimensionality Sweep}
\label{sec:rsweep}

To characterize the effective dimensionality of forgetting, we sweep $r \in \{1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128\}$ at 1.5B (\cref{tab:rsweep}).

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\caption{$r$-sweep (1.5B): variance explained and forgetting $R^2$ by top-$r$ PCA dimensions. Even $r = 1$ captures 96.2\% of variance and $R^2 = 0.854$ of forgetting.}
\label{tab:rsweep}
\begin{tabular}{rcc}
\toprule
$r$ & Var.\ Explained (\%) & Forgetting $R^2$ \\
\midrule
1   & 96.2 & 0.854 \\
2   & 96.4 & 0.854 \\
4   & 96.9 & 0.871 \\
8   & 97.4 & 0.878 \\
16  & 98.0 & 0.879 \\
32  & 98.7 & 0.882 \\
64  & 99.4 & 0.883 \\
128 & 100.0 & 0.884 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Two observations: (1)~a single PCA dimension captures 96.2\% of activation variance, consistent with the concentrated spectrum of transformer activations; (2)~forgetting $R^2$ increases only modestly from $r = 1$ to $r = 128$, suggesting the forgetting-relevant signal is concentrated in very few dimensions.
The ablation-ranked $R^2$ in \cref{tab:h1_results} ($0.933$ for $r = 32$) exceeds the PCA-ranked value here ($0.882$), confirming that ablation importance provides a better ranking than variance explained alone.
