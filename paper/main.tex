\begin{abstract}
Catastrophic forgetting remains a central challenge for continual fine-tuning of large language models.
We propose \textbf{Sparse Feature Preservation} (SFP), a method that identifies a small set of causally important activation feature dimensions via ablation ranking and penalizes drift in those dimensions during fine-tuning.
SFP is grounded in a falsifiable hypothesis: that forgetting is \emph{low-dimensional}---predictable from drift in a few top-ranked activation directions rather than from total representational change.
We formally verify the mathematical core of SFP in Lean~4 with zero \texttt{sorry} axioms, establishing a reduction: if forgetting is bounded by projected drift (our empirical hypothesis H1) and the preservation loss is bounded by $\delta$, then forgetting is bounded by $C\sqrt{\delta}$.
Experiments on Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct in a Math$\to$Code continual learning setting show that top-ranked dimensions predict forgetting with $R^2 = 0.933$, outperforming random ($0.850$) and bottom-ranked ($0.827$) dimensions.
A causal intervention test confirms that perturbing important dimensions causes $4\times$ more performance degradation than perturbing random dimensions.
We report both positive and negative results: the hypothesis fails at 135M parameters (a scale artifact) but holds at 1.5B, and the margin over random controls, while in the correct direction, is modest.
\end{abstract}
