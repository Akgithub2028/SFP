\begin{abstract}
Sequential fine-tuning of large language models causes severe capability regressions on previously learned tasks.
We propose \textbf{Subspace Feature Preservation} (SFP), a principled regularization method that penalizes activation drift within a compact, empirically identified subspace of functional feature dimensions.
SFP is motivated by a falsifiable mechanistic hypothesis (H1): catastrophic forgetting is predictable from drift along a small set of ablation-ranked activation directions rather than from diffuse, whole-model representational change.
We formalize the mathematical core of SFP in Lean~4 using Mathlib with zero \texttt{sorry} axioms.
The central theorem establishes a verified reduction: if H1 holds and the empirical preservation loss is bounded by~$\delta$, task forgetting is strictly bounded by~$C\sqrt{\delta}$.
Experiments on Qwen2.5-1.5B in a Math$\to$Code setting demonstrate that drift in the top-ranked subspace predicts forgetting with $R^2 = 0.933$, consistently outperforming total drift ($0.888$), random subspaces ($0.850$), and bottom-ranked dimensions ($0.827$).
A causal intervention test confirms this finding at 1.5B: injecting perturbation noise into important dimensions at inference causes $2.5\times$ more degradation than random dimensions and $28\times$ more than bottom-ranked dimensions at $1.0\sigma$ ($64\times$ at $2.0\sigma$).
We report both positive and negative findings transparently: correlational H1 fails at 135M scale due to representational entanglement but holds at 1.5B, while causal ranking remains preserved across scales.
Across a 5-task benchmark sequence, SFP Pareto-dominates standard experience replay and distillation baselines.

\vspace{0.8em}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Continual Learning, Large Language Models, Activation Subspace Drift, Representation Alignment, Formal Verification, Lean 4
\end{abstract}
